Every Hour Matters

An Hourly Spatio-Temporal Assessment of Air Quality, Dominant Pollutants, and Potential Population Burden in Belgrade, Serbia

A retrospective analytical presentation of the results of an independent research study: hourly EAQI analysis at 27 automatic monitoring stations (AMS) of the local network of the Belgrade City Institute of Public Health (GZJZ) that carry out continuous fixed measurements in populated areas of the City of Belgrade, 2024–2025.

These 27 AMS are the only measurement sites in the local network that provide the hourly data required for EAQI classification. The semi-automatic and indicative measurement sites of the local network, as well as the stations of the national network operated by SEPA, are not included. Throughout this presentation, “monitoring site” refers to one of these 27 AMS.

Poor+ denotes hours in which the air was in the EAQI category Poor, Very poor or Extremely poor. It is an operational group of categories defined in the study, not an official index category.

This is not a view of current conditions, but a review of what happened during 2024 and 2025. For current air quality, see the portal of the Serbian Environmental Protection Agency (SEPA): vazduh.sepa.gov.rs.

Key findings

A single number does not tell the whole story: the mean PM2.5 concentration explains 27.9% of the variation in Poor+ share across the 27 monitoring sites, while hourly data reveal patterns that no single summary indicator can fully describe.

  • Three different rankings. AMS KCS Vračar, Franše Deperea has the highest Poor+ share, AMS Leštane the longest single episode (132 hours), and AMS JP Pošta Srbije the highest person-hours indicator.
  • The pollutant changes with season and hour. PM2.5 prevails in winter and evening Poor+ hours, O3 in summer Poor+ hours and around midday.
  • Coverage is high but uneven. 98.0% of the population lives within 15 km of one of the analyzed AMS, while the uncovered population is concentrated mainly in Obrenovac city municipality (86.6%).

Findings are taken from the conclusion of the study (Section 6).

What the person-hours indicator means

The person-hours indicator combines two things: how many residents live in the zone of a monitoring site (up to 15 km, assigned to the nearest site) and how many hours the air at that site was in the Poor+ category. The product of the two is the total number of person-hours of adverse conditions.

Example from Table 21 of the study: 141,569 residents live in the zone of AMS JP Pošta Srbije, and the site recorded 3,605 Poor+ hours, so its indicator is 510,356,300, the highest in the network. AMS KCS Vračar, Franše Deperea has the most Poor+ hours (4,780), but only 38,204 residents live in its zone, so with 182,615,504 it ranks only twelfth. The first is the site where poor air coincided with the most people; the second is the site where the air was poor most often.

Population in the table is rounded to whole numbers, so the product of the displayed values may differ slightly from the indicator value.

Areas outside monitoring network coverage

Turn on the 15 km range switch above the map: parts of the City of Belgrade that remain uncolored are more than 15 km from the nearest analyzed AMS.

This finding concerns only the spatial availability of measurements, not the level of pollution in these areas, which remains unknown based on the available data. The 15 km radius is the maximum population assignment distance chosen in the study, not an official representativeness zone. Coverage refers only to the 27 analyzed AMS: other measurement sites (semi-automatic, indicative or national network stations) may exist in these areas, so they are not necessarily without any air quality measurement, but lie beyond the reach of continuous hourly monitoring.

Duration: Poor+ episodes

A Poor+ episode is an uninterrupted run of one or more consecutive valid EAQI hours in the category Poor or worse, with no minimum duration threshold (Section 4.3.7 of the study). In addition to all episodes, night and evening episodes (containing at least one hour between 18:00 and 05:59) and winter episodes (containing at least one hour in December, January or February) can be shown separately. Values are computed from the hourly data and are identical to Tables 13 and 14 of the study.

The Show button takes you to the start of that site's longest episode (of all episodes, or of winter episodes) in the Hour by hour section. The site is marked there with a thick black ring, and a bar above the map shows the start and end of the episode and where you currently are. The Play episode button runs through it and stops automatically at the end. Other sites remain visible, so you can see whether the episode was local or affected a larger part of the city at the same time.

Limitations

  • 27 AMS are covered. The presentation is based on 27 automatic monitoring stations of the GZJZ local network, the only sites in that network with hourly data for the EAQI pollutants. The semi-automatic and indicative sites of the local network and the stations of the national SEPA network are not included, so the results describe conditions at these stations, not across the entire air quality measurement network in Belgrade.
  • Not a compliance assessment. This presentation is not an assessment of regulatory compliance with limit values; that remains within the relevant regulatory procedures.
  • Not a health assessment. The EAQI is an air quality index, not a measure of health risk. Poor+ is an operationally defined group of the categories Poor, Very poor and Extremely poor used in the study, not a separate official index category.
  • The person-hours indicator is an indicator of the spatio-temporal burden on the population, not measured individual exposure or a health effect. It assumes that the air quality category determined at a monitoring site approximately applies to its zone and does not account for population mobility, time spent indoors or the sensitivity of particular groups. The 2024 population distribution was used.
  • O3 is not measured at three monitoring sites (Veliki Crljeni, MZ Ušće Obrenovac and Zemun), so their Poor+ share may be lower than it would be if O3 were available.
  • No meteorological data. Possible explanations for seasonal and diurnal patterns remain consistent with the findings but empirically untested; the patterns do not prove individual emission sources or causal relationships.
  • The base map (municipal boundaries and rivers) is for orientation only.
